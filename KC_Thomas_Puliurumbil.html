പണ്ടേതന്നെ പ്രസിദ്ധമായ കല്ലറയ്ക്കുൽ കുടുംബത്തിന്റെ പേരും പെരുമയും പ്രശസ്തിയും വർദ്ധിപ്പിച്ചവരിൽ ഒട്ടും അപ്രധാനമല്ലാത്ത ഒരു സ്ഥാനമാണു്, “എൻജിനീയർ തോമ്മാച്ചൻ” എന്ന ഓമനപ്പേരിൽ അറിയപ്പെട്ടിരുന്ന റിട്ട: എക്സി: എൻജിനീയർ കെ.സി. തോമസ് B.A., B.E, യ്ക്കുള്ളതു്. സാഹചരൃങ്ങളുടെ സമ്മർദ്ദം കൊണ്ടു് ഇംഗ്ലീഷ് വിദ്യാഭ്യാസത്തിനു സൗകരൃം കുറഞ്ഞിരുന്ന അക്കാലത്തു്, കത്തോലിക്കരിൽ ഉദ്യോഗസ്ഥന്മാർ അപൂർവമായിരുന്നു. അക്കാലത്തു് കത്തോലിക്കരിൽ ആദ്യമായി എക്സിക്യട്ടീവ് എൻജിനീയർ പദവിയിലെത്തിയ ഏകവ്യക്തി തോമ്മാച്ചനായിരുന്നു. കൈക്കൂലി, അഴിമതി ഇവയിലകപ്പെടാതെ സത്യസന്ധമായി തൻകൃതൃങ്ങൾ നിർവഹിച്ചു എന്നുള്ളതു്, തിരുവിതാംകൂറിലെ നാനാജാതി മതസ്ഥരും ഗവണ്മെൻറും ഒരു പോലെ സമ്മതിക്കുന്ന വസ്തുതയാകുന്നു. സുദീർഘമായിട്ടുള്ള രാജ്യ സേവനത്തിനു ശേഷം 1944 ജൂലൈമാസത്തിൽ പെൻഷൻപററി പിരിഞ്ഞു. ഇങ്ങനെയിരിക്കെ 17-8-1944-ൽ കൂടിയ പള്ളി യോഗത്തിൽ പള്ളി പൊളിച്ച് വിശാലമായ ഒരു ദേവാലയമായി പണിയുന്നതിനു തീരുമാനം ചെയ്തു. എൻജിനീയർ തോമ്മാച്ചൻറെ നിയന്ത്രണത്തിലും മേൽനോട്ടത്തിലും അക്കാരൃം നിർവഹിക്കണമെന്നും, അതിനു് അദ്ദേഹത്തോടു് അഭ്യർത്ഥിക്കണമെന്നും യോഗം നിശ്ചയിച്ചു. അദ്ദേഹം അതു് യാതൊരു പ്രതിഫലേഛയും കൂടാതെ ചെയ്തകൊള്ളാമെന്നു സമ്മതിച്ചു. ഇങ്ങനെ നിരന്തര രാഷ്ട്ര സേവനത്തെത്തുടർന്നു്, സാമുദായികസേവനവും തുടങ്ങി. ഉടൻതന്നെ പള്ളി പണി ആരംഭിച്ചു. എല്ലാ ദിവസവും 7 മണിക്കു മുൻപു് വേലക്കാർ ജോലിക്കിറങ്ങുന്നതിനു മുമ്പോ, ജോലി തുടങ്ങുമ്പോഴോ പള്ളിയിൽ വരികയും, കുർബ്ബാനസമയമെങ്കിൽ കുർബ്ബാനകണ്ടശേഷം, പണിയിക്കാൻ നിശ്ചയിച്ചിട്ടുള്ള ഓവർസീയരുടെ ആഫീസിലോ, അല്ലെങ്കിൽ ആശാരിമാരുടെ പണിപ്പുരയിൽ ഒരു തടിക്കഷണത്തിലോ മറേറാ ചെന്നിരുന്നു, അന്നു ചെയ്യേണ്ട ജോലിയേപ്പററി വിശദീകരണം നൽകി അവരെ പഠിപ്പിക്കുന്നതു കാണാം.

“ഒരിക്കൽ ഒരു മാന്യൻ തോമ്മാച്ചനെ കാണാൻ അതിരാവിലെ വീട്ടിൽ ചെന്നന്വേഷിച്ചതിനുശേഷം അവിടെനിന്നും പള്ളിയിൽ വന്നു. ആശാരിയുടെ പണിപ്പുരയിൽ ഒരു തടിക്കഷണത്തിൻറെ പുറത്തിരുന്നു്, ആശാരിക്കു പണിയുടെ വിശദീകരണം നൽകിക്കൊണ്ടിരിക്കുന്നതു കണ്ടു്, “ഹാ......ഹാ…. ഇത്ര രാവിലെ വരുമോ! ഈ ഇരുപ്പും വേഷവും കണ്ടിട്ടു് അവരുടെ കൂട്ടത്തിൽ ഒരു പണിക്കാരനായിരിക്കുമെന്നു് അൽല്പം അകലെവച്ചു് ഞാൻ വിചാരിച്ചു. നന്നാ അടുത്തു വന്നതിനുശേഷമാണ് തിരിച്ചറിഞ്ഞതു്” എന്നു പറഞ്ഞു. “ഞാനും ഇവരുടെ കൂട്ടത്തിലെ ഒരു പണിക്കാരൻ തന്നെ. പക്ഷേ, ഉളിയും കൊട്ടുവടിയും എടുത്തില്ലെന്നേ ഉള്ളു” എന്നു മറുപടിയും പറഞ്ഞു. അനാഡംബരമായ വസ്ത്രധാരണവും ജോലിയിലുള്ള ശുഷ്കാന്തിയും കാണിക്കാൻ ഈ സംഭവം വിവരിച്ചു എന്നേയുള്ളു.
“രാവിലെ ബഡ്കാപ്പിയും കഴിഞ്ഞ് ജോലിയിൽ ബദ്ധശ്രദ്ധനായി ചിലപ്പോഴല്ല പലപ്പോഴും 2 P.M. വരെ ഇരിക്കുന്നതിനിടയിൽ ബ. വികാരിയച്ചൻ കാപ്പി കുടിക്കാൻ ക്ഷണിച്ചാൽ നന്ദിപൂർവം അതുപേക്ഷിച്ചുകളയും. വിശപ്പില്ലാഞ്ഞിട്ടല്ല; അതു് അദ്ദേഹത്തിന്റെ തത്വദീക്ഷയ്ക്കു വിരുദ്ധമായതുകൊണ്ടാണ്. ഇങ്ങനെയിരിക്കെ 15-10-1961-ൽ കൂടിയ പള്ളിയോഗത്തിൽ പള്ളിയുടെ പണികളെല്ലാം നിർത്തിവയ്ക്കണമെന്നു്, തീരുമാനിക്കപ്പെട്ടതോടുകൂടിയാണു് തോമ്മാച്ചൻ തൻറെ ജോലിയിൽനിന്നും പിന്മാറിയതു്. ഇപ്രകാരം നിഷ്കളങ്കമായി യാതൊരു പ്രതിഫലവും പററാതെ സുദീർഘമായ പത്തു വർഷങ്ങളോളം സമുദായസേവനം നടത്തിയ ഈ ത്യാഗിയുടെ സേവന സ്മാരകരേഖകൾ ഈ പള്ളിയുടെ കരിങ്കല്ലിൽ കൊത്തിപ്പോകുന്നതിനു് ആർക്കും കൃതജ്ഞത തോന്നിയില്ലെങ്കിലും, പള്ളിയുടെ ഭിത്തിയിലെ ഓരോ കരിങ്കൽ കഷണവും അതിനു സാക്ഷ്യം വഹിക്കും. നിശ്ചയം.

രാഷ്ട്രീയമൊന്നുമില്ലാത്ത പ്രതിഫലേച്ഛയൊന്നും തീണ്ടാത്ത നിസ്വാർത്ഥ പൊതുപ്രവർത്തകനും, ആർക്കും പൂർണ്ണ വിശ്വാസത്തോടെ സഹായവും ഉപദേശവും എപ്പോഴും ധൈരൃപ്പെട്ട് തേടാവുന്ന ഒരുറവിടവും മാർഗ്ഗദേശിയും ആയിരുന്നു ശ്രീ. കെ.സി. തോമസ്സ്, താൻ കൈകാരൃം ചെയ്യേണ്ടി വന്നിരുന്ന ഏതു കാരൃത്തിലും പരിപൂർണ്ണ അർപ്പണ ബുദ്ധിയും ആത്മാർത്ഥതയും മാത്രം പ്രദർശിപ്പിച്ചിരുന്ന ഒരു യഥാർത്ഥ മനുഷ്യസ്നേഹിയായിരുന്നു ആനത്താനത്തു എൻജിനീയർ തോമ്മാച്ചൻ എന്നു്, അദ്ദേഹത്തെ ഒരിക്കലെങ്കിലും കണ്ടു പരിചയപ്പെടുവാൻ കഴിഞ്ഞവരെല്ലാം സമ്മതിക്കുന്ന ഒരു യാഥാർത്ഥ്യമാണു്.

ഇങ്ങനെ ഒരു മികച്ച പരോപകാരിയും, ഒരുത്തമ ക്രിസ്ത്യാനിയുമായിരുന്ന അദ്ദേഹം ആരോഗൃവാനായി സകുടുംബം താമസിച്ചുവരവെ, 1973 ഫെബ്രുവരി മാസം മുതൽ വാർദ്ധക്യസഹജമായ അസുഖങ്ങൾ അനുഭവപ്പെട്ടു തുടങ്ങുകയും ക്രമേണ അവ മൂർഛിച്ച് കോട്ടയം Caritas ആശുപത്രിയിൽ വച്ചു് 1973 ആഗസ്റ്റ് 15 -നു രാവിലെ 9 മണിക്കു്, അന്ത്യകൂദാശകളെല്ലാം സ്വീകരിച്ചു് നമ്മുടെ കർത്താവിൽ നിദ്രപ്രാപിച്ചു.

Er. K. C. Thomas B.A., B.E. (Thommachan)

Retired Executive Engineer, P.W.D. • Anathanam, Flower Mount ("Pushpamala"), Kanjirappally • (1889 – 1973)

Early Life, Education & Marriage

Shri K. C. Thomas (Thommachan), the 8th child and a 6th-generation member of Branch E (the 5th branch) of the Kallarackal family, born to Kunjuvarkey Kuruvila, was born in the year 1889 A.D.

He completed his education at St. Berchmans High School, Changanacherry; C.M.S. College, Kottayam; and St. Joseph’s College, Trichinopoly (Tiruchirappalli), earning a First-Class B.A. Degree in Mathematics. While subsequently pursuing his B.E. Degree at the College of Engineering, Madras (Guindy), in 1910 A.D., he married Kathreenamma, the second child and eldest daughter of Kalathil Chandyachan of Kanjuparambil, Chennankary. Upon graduating with a B.E. degree in Civil Engineering in 1913 A.D., he attained the proud distinction of becoming the very first Engineer from the Syrian Catholic community in this country/state.

Career in Travancore P.W.D. & Pioneering Bridge Engineering

He subsequently served as an apprentice under the renowned Chief Engineer of the Travancore P.W.D., **Mr. A. H. Bastow**, earning his highest commendation. Before long, in 1915, he joined government service as the Head of the Design Branch in the Chief Engineer’s own office. Mr. Bastow, an acclaimed authority on Reinforced Concrete Design, was exceptionally pleased with the extraordinary talent displayed by Shri K. C. Thomas in that field.

Shri K. C. Thomas prepared novel designs for several large, modern reinforced concrete bridges. When construction was launched, Mr. Bastow personally selected and appointed Shri K. C. Thomas as the Assistant Engineer in charge of the most critical undertaking—the Erappuzha Bridge situated north of Chengannur. Through these pioneering endeavors, it was widely acknowledged at the time that Travancore P.W.D. had secured the leadership position across India among all princely states and British provinces in the construction of reinforced concrete bridges.

Peermade Sub-Division (1922–1927)

From 1922 to 1927, while Shri K. C. Thomas held charge of the Peermade Sub-Division, he initiated and completed the survey, alignment, and construction of several vital new forest-fringe and ghat roads in that high-range terrain.

Trivandrum Wellington Water Works & Principled Integrity

From 1928 to 1933, as the chief designer, engineer, and mastermind from inception to completion of the **Trivandrum Wellington Water Works**—the state's first mega drinking water supply project—he won unanimous, unstinted acclaim from his colleagues, superior officers, and the Government itself. Thereafter, at the special insistence of the then Chief Engineer, **Mr. G. B. E. Truscott**, he served for a period as his Personal Assistant.

Later, while serving as Executive Engineer in Trivandrum, tenders were invited for the large buildings of the State Transport Garage and Central Workshop—a prestigious project of the era. A covert directive was issued from above to award the contract at almost double the P.W.D. estimate to a group favored by a close relative of the Dewan, Sir C. P. Ramaswamy Aiyar. Shri K. C. Thomas steadfastly refused to comply with this irregular instruction and rejected it. As a punitive measure for his refusal to be subservient ("Punishment for non-subservience"), on the pretext that he was an expert in water works, he was abruptly saddled with full responsibility for the Nagercoil Water Works Project and transferred there.

Accepting this without protest, Shri K. C. Thomas assumed charge immediately. Applying his vast expertise gained from the Trivandrum Water Works alongside his natural ingenuity, he substantially completed the primary works of the Nagercoil Water Works within approximately two years. Concurrently, he was also entrusted with the Shencottah Water Works, the major works of which he likewise brought to near-completion during that same timeframe.

The Pallivasal Hydro-Electric Project & Meeting with the Chief Secretary

As these works entered their final stages, he received an urgent communication from the Chief Engineer: > *"It is understood that the Dewanji has directed your immediate transfer to take up the stalled and troubled works of the Pallivasal Hydro-Electric Project. Official government orders follow."*

Shri K. C. Thomas suspected whether this was yet another punitive transfer intended to burden him with insurmountable obstacles—or perhaps an orchestrated maneuver to let him meet his "Waterloo" at the Pallivasal Hydro-Electric Project, an undertaking from which almost everyone else had retreated in frustration.

To clear this doubt, he called upon his long-time friend, the Chief Secretary, Shri Neelakanta Iyer, and asked candidly: > "Swami! Has the Dewan caught hold of me this time to destroy me or to build me up?"

Bursting into laughter, the Chief Secretary replied: > "The Dewan conducted very secret, thorough investigations regarding you and all your past works. In not a single report did he find anything other than unreserved praise! The Pallivasal project is the Dewan’s darling child. He has become completely convinced that there is no one else in this state capable of saving it except you. Therefore, gladly accept this new responsibility, work with the same brilliance you showed in the past, and render true service to the nation. You have all my warmest blessings."

Assured of Shri Neelakanta Iyer’s sincerity, Shri K. C. Thomas departed for Munnar with absolute peace of mind and complete self-confidence.

Achievements at Munnar & Dignified Retirement (1940–1944)

From February 1940, he immersed himself in his new assignment. He systematically resolved every chronic malfunction of the long-paralyzed **Pallivasal Power House**—an issue that had sparked intense public outcry and debates in the Legislative Assembly—and restored it to full operational efficiency. He expeditiously completed the new Head Regulator (subsequently named the **"Ramaswamy Iyer Head Works"**) and the new penstock pipelines.

Thereafter, he carried out the construction of the Kundala Dam and the Mattupetty Dam with remarkable skill and efficiency. However, before their completion, his retirement upon reaching the superannuation age approached in 1944. His superior officer, the Chief Resident Engineer Mr. S. J. Bruford, considering Shri K. C. Thomas’s continued guidance indispensable for the progress of the Kundala and Mattupetty dams, requested that he remain in service and inquired under what terms he would agree to an extension.

Guided solely by patriotism and public welfare, Shri Thomas submitted very modest and reasonable terms. During their discussion, Mr. Bruford agreed that they were thoroughly justified and assured him of government approval. However, Chief Secretary Neelakanta Iyer, who would have readily sanctioned them, had retired. His successor, with a narrow bureaucratic mindset, summarily dismissed Mr. Bruford’s recommendations.

Learning of this, Shri Thomas addressed a fitting rejoinder to the Government. He pointed out that Mr. Bruford could attest that he had never solicited an extension, but had yielded only upon Bruford’s persistent pleading; he had assumed he was dispatched to Munnar because the Government wished to rectify early blunders and prevent their recurrence on similar projects; yet he was astonished by the Government’s "penny-wise policy"; and he therefore requested to be relieved of his duties on the exact date he attained 55 years of age.

Forwarding this letter to the Government, Mr. Bruford wrote again, strongly emphasizing that Shri K. C. Thomas’s services were imperative and that it was in the Government’s own interest to concede his fair terms and grant an extension. However, the Dewan, who had been making strenuous efforts to conceal the early failures, delays, and financial losses of the Pallivasal scheme from the public, took serious offense at Shri Thomas's candid remarks. Miffed, the Dewan issued a government order stating that since Shri Thomas’s letter was deemed to have "gone too far," permission for his retirement was granted with regret. Accordingly, in July 1944, he retired with exemplary honor and dignity.

Refusal of Re-employment & Settlement at "Pushpamala"

Subsequently, the pace of construction at the Kundala and Mattupetty dams slowed drastically. Distressed by this, Mr. Bruford, with the Dewan’s confidential approval, visited Shri K. C. Thomas at his residence and offered to personally negotiate terms with the Dewan for his re-entry into service. But Shri Thomas declined, replying simply that he had no desire to deprive younger engineers of their rightful promotions on his account, while expressing his sincere regrets to Mr. Bruford for not being able to accept.

Following his retirement, Shri K. C. Thomas resided in Kottayam from 1944 to 1946 while constructing his new residence, "Pushpamala" (Flower Mount), at Anathanam in Kanjirappally, where he moved with his family in 1946.

Public Leadership & The Construction of St. Dominic’s Forane Church

Blessed with sound health and deep public-spiritedness, he remained extraordinarily active. He was the very life and soul of: * **The All Kerala Agriculturists Association** * **The Kerala Catholic Trust** * **The First Official P.W.D. Re-organisation Committee**

His selfless public service through these bodies over many years remains unforgettable. Furthermore, despite formidable challenges from 1947 onwards, it was through his tireless decade-long efforts that the magnificent St. Dominic’s Forane Church that stands in Kanjirappally today was brought to substantial completion.

An eyewitness account of his monumental church building work records: > "Among those who enhanced the renown and prestige of the ancient Kallarackal family, a pre-eminent position belongs to Retired Executive Engineer K. C. Thomas B.A., B.E., affectionately known as 'Engineer Thommachan'. In an era when English education was scarce and Catholic officials were rare, Thommachan was the first and only Catholic to attain the rank of Executive Engineer. That he discharged his duties with unimpeachable integrity, untainted by bribery or corruption, is a fact acknowledged alike by all communities and by the Government. > > On August 17, 1944, the parish assembly resolved to demolish the old church and erect a spacious, monumental cathedral-like structure, requesting Engineer Thommachan to undertake its honorary control and supervision. He agreed to do so without accepting any remuneration whatsoever. Thus, following a lifetime of national service, began his dedicated community service. > > Every single day, before 7:00 AM—either before the laborers arrived or just as work commenced—he would be at the church. If it was mass time, he would attend holy mass, and then take a seat on a wooden block in the carpentry workshop or in the overseer's shed, instructing the craftsmen and explaining the day's tasks. > > Once, a gentleman visited his home early in the morning, and not finding him there, came to the church. Seeing him seated on a log in the carpenter's shed explaining work to a carpenter, he remarked: 'Ha! Ha! Coming so early! Seeing your simple attire and seat from afar, I thought you were one of the workmen; only on coming close did I recognize you!' Thommachan replied with a smile: 'I am indeed a worker among them; the only difference is that I have not picked up a chisel and mallet!' > > Having had only bed-tea in the morning, he would work absorbedly, often until 2:00 PM. If the Reverend Vicar invited him for coffee, he would gratefully decline. Not because he lacked hunger, but because accepting hospitality violated his strict personal principles. He stepped down on October 15, 1961, when the parish assembly resolved to pause further works. Though no commemorative plaque was carved in stone to thank this selfless soul who gave a decade of honorary service, every single granite block in the walls of this church bears silent, eternal witness to his sacrifice."

He was a selfless public worker without political ambition or desire for reward—a dependable source of wise counsel and guidance to whom anyone could turn with complete confidence.

Demise

Living as an outstanding philanthropist and a devout Christian, he began to suffer from age-related ailments in February 1973. Gradually worsening, he received all the Last Rites of the Church and fell asleep in the Lord at **9:00 AM on August 15, 1973**, at Caritas Hospital, Kottayam.
